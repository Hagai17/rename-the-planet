# Candidates — Rename the Planet (u-motasimfuad-rename-the-planet-094b0369)

Gate: /output/entry.txt = UTF-8 text, 1–200 chars, one line.
Judge (glm-5.3-flash, comedy editor) weighs, in order:
1. Names a solar-system planet + funny new name + why, same line.
2. Funny on first read; laugh from the idea, not a pun on the real name alone.
3. The why leans on that planet's own traits (heat, rings, size, moons) — fits no other planet.
4. Fresh angle, not the obvious pun.
5. Tight: every word works.

## Drafts
A. Rename Saturn 'The Bath Toy': it's less dense than water, so in a big enough bathtub it would float — rings, moons and all, bobbing next to the duck.
B. Rename Mars 'The Bad Neighbourhood': its only two moons are named Fear and Panic, and every rover we send there eventually stops answering.
C. Rename Neptune 'The Spoiler': mathematicians found it with pencil and paper before any telescope did — the only planet that was homework before it was a place.
D. Rename Mercury 'The Raisin': its core is cooling, so the whole planet is slowly shrinking and wrinkling — ageing faster than its own 88-day years.
E. Rename Jupiter 'The Argument': it has a storm wider than Earth that has been raging for at least 300 years and refuses to settle.
F. Rename Earth 'Ocean': 71% of the surface is water, so we named it after the dirt — a branding decision as solid as the 29% we stand on.
G. Rename Uranus 'The Barrel': knocked clean onto its side, it doesn't orbit the Sun so much as roll around it — 84 years of lying down on the job.
H. Rename Mars 'The Lemon': the whole planet is rusting, and every vehicle we've sent there has broken down — a one-star review with wheels.
I. Rename Mercury 'Monday': its year takes 88 days but one full day lasts 176 — the only planet where Monday outlives the year.
J. Rename Mercury 'The Double Shift': its year runs 88 days, but a single day lasts 176 — you clock in on your birthday and clock out two years older.
K. Rename Earth 'Kevin': every other planet is named after a god; ours is named after the dirt under your shoes. Kevin is at least a promotion.
L. Rename Neptune 'The Hairdryer': its winds reach 2,100 km/h, fastest in the solar system — it has spent 4.5 billion years blowing nobody's hair.

## Duels (judge criteria, in order)
- A vs D: Both single-trait. A's float fact is the most-quoted Saturn fact there is —
  the judge has "read a thousand planet puns"; freshness (crit. 4) hurts A. D's
  shrinking-raisin is less known and the ageing twist lands. D wins.
- B vs H (both Mars): B's Fear-and-Panic moons are the most planet-specific fact in
  the whole set (crit. 3, perfect score) and "stops answering" is a second beat.
  H's rust/lemon is good but the breakdown claim is looser. B wins.
- C vs L (both Neptune): C's homework-before-a-place is a genuinely fresh angle and
  the punchline is an idea, not a number. L is a number plus an absurd image; thinner. C wins.
- I vs J (same Mercury fact): I's "Monday" name collides with a field entry titled
  "Venus is Monday" — same name-word in the field dulls freshness. J keeps the fact,
  adds a bodily clock-in/clock-out scene (winners-study: concrete beats abstract),
  and "two years older" lands the twist. J wins.
- E vs G: E's storm-argument is solid but "storm = long argument" is a familiar
  metaphor. G's rolling barrel is visual and fresh, but "Tipsy" in the field already
  works the tilt. Close; E slightly ahead on first-read laugh.
- F vs K (both Earth): F's ocean-named-dirt observation is a known internet chestnut;
  K's Kevin is fresher and sillier, and the god/dirt contrast is planet-specific.
  K wins, but both are Earth-name jokes, a crowded shelf. Neither reaches the final.
- Semifinal B vs C: B has two stacked planet-only facts and a grim laugh; C has one
  fact and a witty closer. On crit. 2 (funny on first read) B's "moons named Fear
  and Panic" gets an instant laugh from the names alone; C needs a beat to set up.
  B wins narrowly.
- Semifinal J vs D: J's day-outlasts-two-years paradox is the crispest single twist
  in the set, and the birthday clock-out makes it bodily. D is charming but gentler.
  J wins.
- Final B vs J: Both trait-locked and tight. B: two facts, laugh front-loaded on the
  moon names, second laugh on dead rovers. J: one fact, laugh back-loaded on the
  clock-out twist. Criterion 2 says the name and why must land together on the first
  read — B's name ("Bad Neighbourhood") only pays off once the moons land, which is
  immediate; J's name pays off at the very end. B's double-beat gives the judge two
  chances to laugh; J's single paradox is cleaner but riskier if the day/year fact
  doesn't register instantly. Verdict: B by a hair (margin ~1–2).
